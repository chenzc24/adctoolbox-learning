• Not well suited for high resolution (10-bit)
   – Require large capacitor
       • Problematic for input and reference driving
       • Large DAC switching power
   – Require low-noise comparator
       • Require large power consumption
• Conversion rate is limited
   – Require N conversion cycles for N-bit
   – DAC RC settling, comparator delay, SAR logic delay

                     Equipartition Theorem
• Equipartition theorem in statistical physics states:
   – In thermal equilibrium each degree of freedom holds on average thermal
     energy of kT/2
• In an electronic circuit, each independent capacitor holds an electric energy of
  kT/2 and each independent inductor holds a magnetic energy of kT/2.
• This theorem can help reduce the complexity of noise analysis
   – But it does not apply to active circuits that are not in equilibrium

                        DAC noise
                            • DAC noise PSD is set by Rsw
                            • DAC settling BW is set by CDAC
                            • DAC noise BW is set by CP
                               – 𝐶𝐷𝐴𝐶 ≫ 𝐶𝑃

                              which is large, but not all of it affects
                              the comparator decision
                            • For DAC BW >> comparator BW, the
                              majority of the DAC noise is filtered out
                               – Comparator BW ~ DAC settling BW
                            • Best way to suppress DAC noise is to
                              make Rsw small
                               – Consume low power
                               – Also speed up DAC settling
                               – Benefit from CMOS scaling 20

                 Comparator noise analysis
• For a well-optimized comparator, we prefer:
   – Large G: attenuate the noise from the latch stage
        • In practice, G is around 5
        • A too-large G slows down the comparator
             – Regeneration is faster than integration
   – Large gm/ID: it improves energy efficiency
        • In practice, gm/ID is around 15 V-1
        • A too-large gm/ID slows down the comparator; it also increases the
          comparator input capacitance, which causes signal attenuation and
          leads to increase ADC input referred noise
• Offset analysis is very similar to noise

  Low-noise low-power comparator design
• Transistor level (generally applicable):
   – Enlarge gm/ID
   – Enlarge preamplifier gain
   – Incomplete discharge of the integration capacitor
   – Reuse current (CMOS input)


• Architectural level (enabled by SAR’s property & redundancy):
   – Comparator noise switching
   – Majority voting
   – Statistical estimation
   – Adaptive tracking average
