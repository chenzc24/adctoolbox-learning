      How Fast Can a Pipelined ADC Go? (1)

• Non-dominant pole in two-stage amplifier hard to move past fT/5
• For 73 phase margin (optimum for fast settling), loop crossover
  frequency is 1/3 of non-dominant pole frequency
• Settling linearly to 0.1% precision takes 7 loop time constants;
  typically budget ~10 time constants
• Ideally, we'd have 1/2 clock cycle to settle linearly, but there is some
  time needed for slewing and non-overlap clock timing
   – Assume 60% of half cycle is available for linear settling
• In summary (here we have assume that the feedback factor is 1)

                    Time Interleaved ADC




• Idea: interleave 4 ADCs so that each ADC runs as fs/4
• Originally, (SH + Conversion) needs to finish within ts
• Now, both the SH and conversion speed are relaxed.
   – Note that SH has to finish within ts regardless of how many ADCs are
     interleaved

                  Issues with Time Interleaving

•   Offset mismatch
     – Channels may have different offsets
     – Output will contain a periodic error sequence that manifest itself as spurs in
        the output spectrum
•   Gain mismatch
     – Channels may also have different gains
     – Results in amplitude modulation
•   Clock skew
     – Hard to guarantee precise phase relationship between individual clocks
     – Results in phase modulation (similar to aperture uncertainty)
•   Solutions
     – "Careful design"
     – Analog or digital calibration
          • See e.g. [Jamal, JSSC 12/2002]
