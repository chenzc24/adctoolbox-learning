• What it means:
   – How much power does it take for an ADC to convert 1 bit?
• FOM2 is the most widely used ADC FOM
• Typically, circuits power and speed are proportional to each other
   – fs  gm/C  Power
   – FOM2 captures this tradeoff correctly
• What about power vs. ENOB?
   – 1 extra bit = 2x power?
                                                                          5
                      ENOB vs. Power
• For thermal noise limited ADCs:
   – 1 extra bit → 4x less noise power → 4x C
   – Power  gm  C → 1 extra bit = 4x Power
• For mismatch limited ADCs:
   – 1 extra bit → 2x comparator #
   – 1 extra bit → ½ offset = 4x size = 4x power
   – In total, 1 extra bit = 8x power
• There is no fixed relationship between ENOB and power
• An alternative solution is that
   – We drop ENOB from FOM2 and only compare ADCs with similar
      ENOB

                Design Space Partitioning

• High SNR
   – Complexity ~1 (e.g. first integrator in sigma-delta ADC)
   – Limited by thermal noise
• Moderate SNR
   – Complexity ~Bits (e.g. pipelined ADC)
   – Partly limited by thermal noise
• Low SNR
   – Complexity ~2Bits (e.g. flash ADC)
   – Limited by matching, quantization noise

                       CMOS Scaling
• CMOS scaling brings mixed news
   – Good news: Lower dynamic power, faster speed, higher gm/ID
     given the same speed, …
   – Bad news: Lower VDD, lower transistor intrinsic gain, …
• New technologies brings new opportunities
   – Vacuum tubes → Bipolar → CMOS → FINFET, nanowire,
     graphene
• What should we do?
   – Invent new topologies that take advantage of CMOS scaling and
     obviate its drawbacks
   – Digitally assisted analog circuit design
       • Minimalistic analog circuits
       • Digital compensation of resulting non-idealities
   – Time/phase domain analog signal processing
