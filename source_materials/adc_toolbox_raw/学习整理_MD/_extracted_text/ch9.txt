                   Reducing Complexity

• How much power would a 8-bit flash ADC consume?
   – A 6-bit 1GS/s flash ADC consumes about 500mW (in 0.35um)
   – To increase the resolution to 8-bit, we need to
      • Increase the number of comparators by 4
      • Increase size & power of each comparator by 42 (matching)
   – ADC Power: 500mW·43 = 32W (!)
• How to increase resolution w/o significantly increasing power?
   – Interpolation
   – Folding
   – Folding & interpolation
   – Multi-step conversion, pipelining

                        Interpolation
• Reduce # preamps by interpolating preamp outputs
   – Lower power dissipation
   – Lower input capacitance
   – Lower area
• Same number of latches
• Important “side-benefit”
   – Lower offset through offset averaging

                  Issues with Interpolation

• Speed penalty
   – Resistor ladder resistance
   – Latch capacitance and parasitic capacitance
• Preamp nonlinearity
   – For interpolation factors >2, amplifier nonlinearity can limit the
     precision of zero crossings
   – Must ensure that "linear range" of adjacent preamplifiers overlaps
• Upper bound on interpolation factor, typically around 4
• Interpolation factor of 2 is widely used
   – No need for resistor string, low design complexity
• Details see [van de Plassche, p.121]

           Folding ADC Problems & Solutions

• Dynamic problems
   – Frequency at the folder output is approximately input frequency times
     folding factor!
   – Hard to match the delay through coarse/fine signal paths
   – Solution: add track-and-hold
• Static problems
   – Offsets in folder transistors can cause DNL and INL
   – Interpolation with a factor greater than 2 can introduce DNL and INL
      due to amplifier nonlinearity
   – Solutions: averaging or calibration
